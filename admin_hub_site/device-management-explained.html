device-management-explained

Mobile device management, or MDM, sounds like surveillance to users and like a project to admins. It is neither: it is the remote control for the handful of settings that decide whether a lost phone is an annoyance or a breach.

what it does

  • It puts settings on a device over the air: the passcode rule, Wi-Fi, mail accounts, VPN, certificates.
  • It installs and removes company apps.
  • It reports the state of the device: system version, encrypted or not, last contact.
  • It can lock, locate and wipe a device, or remove only the company's part.
  • It can refuse access to mail and files from devices that do not meet the rules. That last point is what makes it a security tool instead of an inventory.

how a device gets in

  • Automatic enrolment is the goal for company-owned devices: the device is tied to your management before it is unboxed, and a factory reset does not remove it. A stolen device stays useless.
  • Manual enrolment, by installing an app or scanning a code, works for devices you already have and for private ones. The user can undo it.
  • Devices bought in a shop are not registered to you. Apple devices can be added afterwards with a second device and some patience, others often not. Buy through a reseller that registers them.

  • Tell users this before enrolment, in writing. Most resistance to device management is the fear of being watched.
  • In some countries, employee representatives have a say before such a system is introduced. Ask first.

a small set of policies to start with

  • A passcode of six digits or more and automatic lock after a few minutes.
  • Encryption on, which the passcode brings with it on current phones.
  • A minimum system version, raised a few weeks after each major release.
  • Company mail, calendar and files only in managed apps, and no copying from those into private apps.
  • Remote wipe of the company's data when a device is lost or someone leaves.
  • Block devices that are jailbroken or rooted.
  • Notebooks: disk encryption with the key stored centrally, a firewall, automatic updates, no local admin rights.

Start with these, for everyone, and add more only when something calls for it. A long policy list that half the devices fail on day one teaches people to look for ways round it.

choosing one

  • If you have Microsoft 365 Business Premium or E3, Intune is included. Use it before buying something else.
  • A company that runs on Apple devices is well served by one of the Apple-only management services, which are simpler.
  • Google Workspace includes basic management for phones.
  • Whatever you choose: enrol three test devices of each kind first, lose one on purpose, and practise the lock and the wipe.
