rules of thumb

  • Separate by trust, not by department. Servers, clients, guests, phones, printers and "things" (cameras, building control, TVs) each get a segment, because they deserve different firewall rules.
  • Guests reach the internet and nothing else. The guest segment must not reach any internal address, including the management interfaces of your own devices.
  • Management lives alone. Switches, access points, hypervisors and out-of-band interfaces in one VLAN that only admin workstations can reach.
  • Avoid the popular ranges. 192.168.0.0/24, 192.168.1.0/24 and 192.168.178.0/24 are in millions of homes. VPN users with the same range at home cannot reach yours.
  • Readable beats tight. With a 10.x.0.0/16 there are 256 /24 networks. Spending one per VLAN, with the VLAN ID in the address, saves more time in troubleshooting than tight subnets ever save in addresses.
  • Write it down where the next person finds it. The plan is only useful while it matches reality.

A VLAN separates nothing by itself: traffic between VLANs is allowed or blocked by the device that routes between them. Put the gateways on a firewall, or use access lists on the switch that routes. IPv4 only. The generated configuration is a starting point: read it before you paste it.
