label-maker

Labels for the patch panel, the switch, the cable under the desk and the server in the rack. One line per label, a vertical bar for the next line on the same label. Choose the sheet or the tape, add a QR code if you like, and print. The QR codes are made on this page by its own encoder: no library, no service, nothing leaves your browser.

printing that comes out right

  • In the print dialog, set the scale to 100 % (or "actual size") and the margins to none. "Fit to page" moves every label by a few millimetres.
  • Print one sheet on plain paper first and hold it against the label sheet in front of a window. If it is off, check the sheet type on the packet: the numbers in the list are the common European Avery formats.
  • Label tape: choose the tape here, then pick the tape printer and the matching tape width in the dialog. Each label is one page, the length you set here.
  • A partly used sheet goes back in: count the missing labels from the top left and enter the number under "skip".

a labelling scheme that lasts

  • Name both ends of a cable the same way: where it starts and where it ends, for example SW1 Gi1/0/1 | PP-A 01. Whoever pulls it in five years reads both on either end.
  • Patch panel ports: panel letter and port number with leading zero (PP-A 01 to PP-A 48), so lists sort in the right order.
  • Devices: hostname first in bold, then the address and the place. The QR code can link to the entry in your documentation or wiki with the template.
  • Use self-laminating cable labels or tape for cables. Paper labels on a cable come off within a year.
  • Write the scheme down once, in the IT documentation, and the next person keeps it.
