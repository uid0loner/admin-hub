dmarc-report-reader

Once you publish a DMARC record, the big mail providers send you a report every day: an XML file nobody wants to read. Drop a day's or a month's worth here. You get the list of everyone who sends mail with your domain in the From line, what passed and what failed, which of your own services are not set up yet, and whether you can tighten the policy. The files are read by your browser and never uploaded.

getting the reports

Receivers send reports to the address in the rua tag of your DMARC record. If you have no record yet, this one changes nothing about how your mail is delivered and only asks for reports:

_dmarc.example.org.  TXT  "v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc-reports@example.org"
  • Use a mailbox of its own. A busy domain gets dozens of reports a day.
  • The first reports arrive after one to two days. Collect two weeks before you draw conclusions: some senders only run monthly.
  • Save the attachments into a folder and drop them all at once. Duplicates are recognised.
  • If the reports should go to an address in another domain, that domain has to publish a record that allows it, or receivers will not send them.

Fails: forwardedYour mail, forwarded by the recipient to another mailbox.Nothing, apart from signing everything with DKIM.

The page reads what receivers reported, and they only report on mail they received themselves. A sender that never mailed a reporting provider does not appear. The classification is a reading aid: whether an unknown address is a forger or your own scanner is something only you can find out. Aggregate reports contain no message content and no recipient addresses. Related: domain check for your SPF, DKIM and DMARC records, mail flow debugger, mail flow cheat sheet, phish analyzer.
