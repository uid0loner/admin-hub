password-strength

Type a password or passphrase to see its entropy and estimated crack time under different attack speeds. The analysis runs entirely in your browser: nothing you type is transmitted, logged or stored, not even locally. The optional breach check further down is the one exception, and it only happens if you press its button.

known breaches

Has this exact password appeared in a published data breach? The check uses the Pwned Passwords range API: your browser hashes the password with SHA-1 and sends only the first 5 of the 40 hash characters to api.pwnedpasswords.com. The service returns every hash with that prefix, several hundred of them, and the comparison happens here. The password and its full hash never leave this page.

Crack-time estimates are rough and assume no reuse of this password elsewhere, no pattern an attacker could guess (keyboard walks, dates, dictionary words with substitutions), and that the attacker only tries brute force. A long, unique passphrase is usually both stronger and easier to remember than a short complex password. For generating one, see the password generator.
