storage-upgrade-planner

The cheapest upgrade with the largest effect, and the one where the wrong part gets ordered most often. Say what is in the machine and what you want: you get what to buy, what to watch for, and the steps to move the system over without losing anything.

M.2: the notches and the lengths

  • The service manual of the PC or board says what the slot takes: "PCIe/NVMe", "SATA" or both. That line decides, not the look of the slot.
  • The second M.2 slot of a board is often slower, or shares its lanes with SATA ports or a PCIe slot.
  • The short slot with two small antenna plugs next to it is for the Wi-Fi card (key E). No drive goes there.

2.5 inch: one plug, three heights

  • Keep the frame, the rubber rails and the little adapter cable of the old notebook drive. They are not in the box of the new one.
  • SATA is hot-pluggable on paper. Shut the machine down anyway.

find out what is in the machine

wmic diskdrive get model,size,interfacetype        # Windows, old but quick
Get-PhysicalDisk | Format-Table FriendlyName,MediaType,BusType,Size,HealthStatus
lsblk -d -o NAME,SIZE,ROTA,TRAN,MODEL              # Linux: ROTA 1 = hard disk, TRAN = sata or nvme
sudo smartctl -a /dev/sda                           # health: paste the output into the SMART reader

The planner gives a direction, not a compatibility guarantee: the service manual of the device has the last word, and servers want drives from the maker's list. Sizes are rounded up to leave about a third free. Back up before any of this.
